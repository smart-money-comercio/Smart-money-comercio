# Smart Money news editor

The daily brief now selects up to three current sourced stories, alongside the dated market snapshot and upcoming calendar. It uses six inspectable stages within the existing daily agent: Scan, Source Check, Relevance, Selection, Editorial Review and Decision Log. These are bounded role-specific checks; no additional model subscription is required.

Company candidates come from active special-alert evidence and cached original publisher feeds and release indexes. Coverage uses configured core companies plus dated top opportunities. Source dates remain original publication times, never the time a page was fetched. The reader accepts at most six-hour-old feed fetches and 24-hour-old articles, checks HTTPS source links, and rejects HTML page-title fallbacks. Publisher feeds retain publisher authority only when article links match the publisher. Yahoo headlines stay headline-only accounts; they do not establish company confirmation.

Editorial rules version 7 require an original-source company or agency report and a material investor topic. The editor checks both publisher provenance and the original link's issuer/agency domain; an `official` flag alone is insufficient. Company topics include results, guidance, significant products, contracts, acquisitions, capital actions, leadership, operations, regulation and investment. Macro topics include monetary policy, economic releases, energy and public finance. Routine company tutorials and earnings-date announcements do not fill news slots. Secondary headline reports remain held for original-source confirmation, and opinion, price bets, stock-pick roundups and retrospective profiles are skipped. These are bounded editorial checks; company claims remain attributed to the company.

The normal news refresh includes Nvidia, Microsoft, Broadcom, AeroVironment, Kratos, CrowdStrike, Tesla, Apple and Lockheed Martin publisher feeds, Palantir's existing public release service, TSMC and Northrop Grumman release indexes, and the Federal Reserve, Treasury, EIA and DARPA sources. Company stories require the issuer in the monitored universe; agency stories require the original agency domain and a material development. A configured reader does not guarantee a working endpoint or an admissible publication clock. Blocked pages, changed indexes and missing original times remain explicit coverage gaps.

Palantir's naive service dates only identify pages worth checking. At most three exact issuer pages are read for matching full-title publication metadata; the service time is not assigned a guessed timezone. The same bounded metadata check applies to release indexes. RSS and Atom are supported; Atom `updated` is not substituted for an original `published` time. Reading or rendering the editor still makes no extra fetch or channel send.

Refresh uses at most four publisher workers. Cache updates are atomic and serialized, HTTP failures retain a bounded retry deadline, responses are capped at two megabytes, and the editor's source cache is capped at twelve megabytes. Forced refresh respects an active error backoff. Successful company or agency fetches with old reports remain distinguishable from failed fetches or unverified publication clocks. Legacy news intelligence also excludes old or undated original reports from current news inputs.

Selection favors original reports and research relevance, then newer publication times. Fresh primary candidates are scanned before old publications and unconfirmed leads when the 180-candidate log reaches its bound. Duplicate article URLs and normalized titles are skipped. Tracking parameters do not make a repeated article new. Each represented company receives at most one story; Alphabet share classes count as one issuer. Selection reserves room for an eligible broad-market story and a defense/innovation story before filling the remaining slots by research priority. When company candidates are available, at most one broad-market macro story and one defense macro story can be represented. Zero, one or two stories is a valid result. The editor never fills the three-story maximum with opinion.

The editor reads complete titles from the existing RSS cache instead of the compact 180-character display title. It retains normalized original wording up to 1,000 characters; it never silently slices a selected headline at 160 characters. Oversized titles, explicit shortening flags and titles ending in an ellipsis are held. Older alert evidence at the unmarked 240-character title limit is treated as potentially shortened unless a separate original title is available. Whole stories can be dropped for narration length, with the decision logged. Other consumers keep their existing compact feed displays. Stored editions from earlier rule versions remain readable.

The spoken text retains source attribution, original publication time and confirmation status within each story paragraph. Repeated research-focus prompts remain in the evidence log. The brief's "Our view" outlook is conditional and uses the dated snapshot; it does not assert the cause of market moves. Length budgeting removes whole paragraphs; a story removed to fit 3,800 characters is logged as skipped. Final review checks selected story paragraphs against the source-derived statements before returning the report. Quality validation also rejects an unqualified headline paragraph even when another paragraph retains its qualification.

`/editorstatus` shows the latest built edition and all six stages. `/storylog` shows selected, skipped and held candidates from the latest three editions. `/storylog NVDA` filters the decision history to a company. Each candidate retains its headline, original date, source check, original URL, confidence label and selection reason. Market evidence and the underlying research/report hashes link the brief to its inputs. These records describe built editions; Telegram delivery remains in the existing delivery records.

Status and history now summarize the actual reasons candidates were held: an original-source confirmation is needed, the publication is older than 24 hours, the observation is stale, the publication clock is outside the allowed window, the original timezone is missing, the title is incomplete, or the publisher/link fails validation. Company-filtered history counts only that company's candidates. Source-fetch and calendar coverage issues remain separate from candidate holds. These diagnostics distinguish missing coverage from rejected evidence; zero selected stories does not establish that no material news occurred. The source-age and confirmation requirements are unchanged.

`/editorstatus` additionally records each original source's availability, observed time, dated and fresh report counts, newest verified publication date and coverage reason for that exact edition. A healthy feed with zero current reports is available; a failed or unverified reader is limited. Fresh counts precede editorial materiality and selection, and do not promise a selected story. `/storylog SYMBOL` shows the company's publisher coverage even when it has no candidate decisions. Monitored names without a direct reader are identified explicitly. The daily agent's News Intelligence step includes an original-source availability summary after the existing refresh.

Records live in `data/news_editor.sqlite3`, overridable with `NEWS_EDITOR_STATE_FILE`, and retain up to 200 editions from 30 days. Reads use SQLite read-only mode and do not create a missing database. A failed audit write prevents the report build from succeeding. The daily agent additionally checks that the returned report matches a recorded passing editorial review. Deployment preflight uses an isolated temporary editor database.

This update supplies news research and decision inspection. Position sizing, broker order execution and return attribution are separate future workflows. Scoring formulas and alert delivery receipts are unchanged; fresh original reports can feed the existing news inputs. Historical source observations and original publication times remain distinct.

Analyst narration now stores the admitted headline context used for sentiment and portfolio questions. It is rebuilt for each retained story subset before the edition is recorded. This prevents a dropped headline from leaving a trade-policy, geopolitical or defense interpretation behind. Final review matches that context against the actual selected source decisions as well as the dated research report.

Company stories now carry an event and stage review: completed versus proposed acquisitions, terminated contracts, funded amounts versus contract ceilings, demonstrations versus production, and results versus guidance. Each story separates the publisher's wording and disclosed facts from the analyst's interpretation and next evidence checks. Missing deal terms, synergies, orders and earnings effects are not inferred. `/storylog SYMBOL` retains the event stage, evidence scope, source text and hash, extracted fact passages, interpretation and follow-up. Same-edition review rejects altered facts or interpretations from a dropped story.

During the existing refresh, each company RSS/Atom publisher can supply at most one additional fresh release page. Its issuer URL, complete headline and original publication clock must match the admitted feed item. Pages without timestamp metadata require the exact headline and explicit matching release date; the original zoned RSS clock remains authoritative. Only identified article bodies are read, excluding scripts, navigation and boilerplate. A missing or mismatched page leaves the original feed excerpt as the available evidence. Rendering remains cache-only, and neither publication nor observation freshness rules are relaxed.
