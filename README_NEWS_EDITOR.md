# Smart Money news editor

The original publisher registry includes Intel's linked investor RSS endpoint and ASML's canonical investor-relations release index. Exact release identity, original publication clocks and original bodies remain required. Intel's single detail read prefers a fresh material development when a newer calendar or community item would otherwise consume it; ASML remains bounded to three detail pages. Source refresh alone performs these requests. The hourly model, search and original-read limits do not change.

Editor status shows up to five fresh original-report decisions, including reasons for skipped original items. A fresh publisher item can be a calendar notice, community feature or a story outside the configured material event categories; it is not automatically a market-moving development. EIA solar, battery-storage, electricity and natural-gas reports now fall under energy coverage. The software does not infer a price effect from those topics.

New reader editions freeze the validated report, research text and spoken companion in the existing edition record. `/voiceover` and `scripts/export_daily_voiceover.py` revalidate the snapshot before returning narration; they make no research or audio API calls. The text report remains fully attributed. See [README_ELEVENLABS.md](README_ELEVENLABS.md) for the existing renderer handoff.

The daily brief now selects up to three current sourced stories, alongside the dated market snapshot and upcoming calendar. It uses six inspectable stages within the existing daily agent: Scan, Source Check, Relevance, Selection, Editorial Review and Decision Log. These are bounded role-specific checks; no additional model subscription is required.

Company candidates come from active special-alert evidence and cached original publisher feeds and release indexes. Coverage uses configured core companies plus dated top opportunities. Source dates remain original publication times, never the time a page was fetched. The reader accepts at most six-hour-old feed fetches and 24-hour-old articles, checks HTTPS source links, and rejects HTML page-title fallbacks. Publisher feeds retain publisher authority only when article links match the publisher. Yahoo headlines stay headline-only accounts; they do not establish company confirmation.

Editorial rules version 9 require an original-source company or agency report and a material investor topic. The editor checks both publisher provenance and the original link's issuer/agency domain; an `official` flag alone is insufficient. Company topics include results, guidance, significant products, contracts, acquisitions, capital actions, leadership, operations, regulation and investment. Macro topics include monetary policy, economic releases, energy and public finance. Routine company tutorials and earnings-date announcements do not fill news slots. Secondary headline reports remain held for original-source confirmation, and opinion, price bets, stock-pick roundups and retrospective profiles are skipped. These are bounded editorial checks; company claims remain attributed to the company.

The normal news refresh includes Nvidia, Microsoft, Broadcom, AeroVironment, Kratos, CrowdStrike, Tesla, Apple and Lockheed Martin publisher feeds, Palantir's existing public release service, TSMC and Northrop Grumman release indexes, and the Federal Reserve, Treasury, EIA and DARPA sources. Alternate original routes cover Broadcom's financial-news release index, AeroVironment's corporate feed and Kratos's corporate newsroom. Each original IR feed keeps its own coverage status, so a blocked feed remains visible alongside an available alternate. Company stories require the issuer in the monitored universe; agency stories require the original agency domain and a material development. A configured reader does not guarantee a working endpoint or an admissible publication clock. Blocked pages, changed indexes and missing original times remain explicit coverage gaps.

Broadcom and Kratos indexes read at most three exact issuer release pages. Both require a full original title, a zoned publication clock and an identified release body before entering the brief. Kratos's WebPage metadata is accepted only for a specific corporate newsroom article whose URL, metadata identity, publisher title and unique visible heading agree. Modification times and the newsroom index's own date cannot date an article. AeroVironment's corporate feed retains the original RSS clock and complete feed excerpt. Quiet historical reports stay separate from fresh news. The additional routes use the existing refresh, cache and independent-review handoff; they add no model requests or automatic alert subscriptions.

Palantir's naive service dates only identify releases worth checking. At most three publication pages are read. An explicit Business Wire link in that issuer release can identify the exact wire record; its complete title and zoned original publication time must match. The wire redirect must retain that release ID. Conflicting IDs, mismatched titles and old, future or naive clocks remain held. Without a wire link, the reader checks the exact issuer page. Business Wire does not become a general company authority. The issuer report URL and separate publication-clock source remain in the decision log; no timezone is assigned to the naive service date.

Treasury uses its official dated press-release index rather than the retired RSS endpoint. Each clock belongs to its own listing row. Refresh reads at most three current releases, requiring the listed title and zoned publication time to match the exact release page and its identified news body. Missing, conflicting or redirected release evidence is withheld; an unread index headline alone cannot enter the brief. Old releases remain dated history. Historical proofs keep their original source identity without re-enabling the retired endpoint.

RSS and Atom remain supported. Atom `updated`, modification times, date-only metadata and fetch times never replace an original zoned publication time. Update-only feeds are identified as limited coverage. Reading or rendering the editor still makes no extra fetch or channel send.

Refresh uses at most four publisher workers. Cache updates are atomic and serialized, HTTP failures retain a bounded retry deadline, responses are capped at two megabytes, and the editor's source cache is capped at twelve megabytes. Forced refresh respects an active error backoff. Successful company or agency fetches with old reports remain distinguishable from failed fetches or unverified publication clocks. Legacy news intelligence also excludes old or undated original reports from current news inputs.

Selection favors original reports and research relevance, then newer publication times. Fresh primary candidates are scanned before old publications and unconfirmed leads when the 180-candidate log reaches its bound. Duplicate article URLs and normalized titles are skipped. Tracking parameters do not make a repeated article new. Each represented company receives at most one story; Alphabet share classes count as one issuer. Event-aware editions reserve the first slot for the highest-priority admitted story, then seek broad-market and defense/innovation coverage. Fresh original earnings, guidance and regulatory reports with source text can lead; a matching rechecked large move adds attention without establishing its cause. When company candidates are available, at most one broad-market macro story and one defense macro story can be represented. Zero, one or two stories is a valid result. The editor never fills the three-story maximum with opinion.

The editor reads complete titles from the existing RSS cache instead of the compact 180-character display title. It retains normalized original wording up to 1,000 characters; it never silently slices a selected headline at 160 characters. Oversized titles, explicit shortening flags and titles ending in an ellipsis are held. Older alert evidence at the unmarked 240-character title limit is treated as potentially shortened unless a separate original title is available. Whole stories can be dropped for narration length, with the decision logged. Other consumers keep their existing compact feed displays. Stored editions from earlier rule versions remain readable.

The spoken text retains source attribution, original publication time and confirmation status within each story paragraph. Repeated research-focus prompts remain in the evidence log. The brief's "Our view" outlook is conditional and uses the dated snapshot; it does not assert the cause of market moves. Length budgeting removes whole paragraphs; a story removed to fit 3,800 characters is logged as skipped. Final review checks selected story paragraphs against the source-derived statements before returning the report. Quality validation also rejects an unqualified headline paragraph even when another paragraph retains its qualification.

`/editorstatus` shows the latest built edition and all six stages. `/storylog` shows selected, skipped and held candidates from the latest three editions. `/storylog NVDA` filters the decision history to a company. Each candidate retains its headline, original date, source check, original URL, confidence label and selection reason. Market evidence and the underlying research/report hashes link the brief to its inputs. These records describe built editions; Telegram delivery remains in the existing delivery records.

Status and history now summarize the actual reasons candidates were held: an original-source confirmation is needed, the publication is older than 24 hours, the observation is stale, the publication clock is outside the allowed window, the original timezone is missing, the title is incomplete, or the publisher/link fails validation. Company-filtered history counts only that company's candidates. Source-fetch and calendar coverage issues remain separate from candidate holds. These diagnostics distinguish missing coverage from rejected evidence; zero selected stories does not establish that no material news occurred. The source-age and confirmation requirements are unchanged.

`/editorstatus` additionally records each original source's availability, observed time, dated and fresh report counts, newest verified publication date and coverage reason for that exact edition. A healthy feed with zero current reports is available; a failed or unverified reader is limited. Fresh counts precede editorial materiality and selection, and do not promise a selected story. `/storylog SYMBOL` shows the company's publisher coverage even when it has no candidate decisions. Monitored names without a direct reader are identified explicitly. The daily agent's News Intelligence step includes an original-source availability summary after the existing refresh.

Records live in `data/news_editor.sqlite3`, overridable with `NEWS_EDITOR_STATE_FILE`, and retain up to 200 editions from 30 days. Reads use SQLite read-only mode and do not create a missing database. A failed audit write prevents the report build from succeeding. The daily agent additionally checks that the returned report matches a recorded passing editorial review. Deployment preflight uses an isolated temporary editor database.

This update supplies news research and decision inspection. Position sizing, broker order execution and return attribution are separate future workflows. Scoring formulas and alert delivery receipts are unchanged; fresh original reports can feed the existing news inputs. Historical source observations and original publication times remain distinct.

Analyst narration now stores the admitted headline context used for sentiment and portfolio questions. It is rebuilt for each retained story subset before the edition is recorded. This prevents a dropped headline from leaving a trade-policy, geopolitical or defense interpretation behind. Final review matches that context against the actual selected source decisions as well as the dated research report.

Company stories now carry an event and stage review: completed versus proposed acquisitions, terminated contracts, funded amounts versus contract ceilings, demonstrations versus production, and results versus guidance. Each story separates the publisher's wording and disclosed facts from the analyst's interpretation and next evidence checks. Missing deal terms, synergies, orders and earnings effects are not inferred. `/storylog SYMBOL` retains the event stage, evidence scope, source text and hash, extracted fact passages, interpretation and follow-up. Same-edition review rejects altered facts or interpretations from a dropped story.

During the existing refresh, each company RSS/Atom publisher can supply at most one additional fresh release page. Its issuer URL, complete headline and original publication clock must match the admitted feed item. Pages without timestamp metadata require the exact headline and explicit matching release date; the original zoned RSS clock remains authoritative. Only identified article bodies are read, excluding scripts, navigation and boilerplate. A missing or mismatched page leaves the original feed excerpt as the available evidence. Rendering remains cache-only, and neither publication nor observation freshness rules are relaxed.

The news edition leads with retained original-source news before research rankings. Editorial categories set a transparent reading order: policy and economic releases, guidance and regulation, then results, corporate transactions and workforce changes ahead of general product/contract updates. This is an editorial ordering rule, not a measured forecast of price impact. Coverage diversity and all admission checks remain in force. When the character budget removes a story, its analysis is rebuilt from the retained subset. The edition records its lead story ID.

Voluntary exit/retirement offers and announced job reductions receive workforce analysis, covering participation, severance costs, savings targets and timing, service capacity and guidance. A voluntary offer is not a count of employees who departed. Recognized disclosed cost estimates and annual savings targets retain those labels; missing financial terms stay absent. Evidence schema 2 adds the workforce classifier; earlier schema 1 profiles retain their original classification when read from history.

Verified IPO milestones from the independent issuer monitor can enter the news edition before the issuer has a trading ticker. The SEC/issuer identity, excerpt hash, original acceptance/publication clock, observation clock and stage must validate. A generated filing summary is identified as a filing record, rather than quoted as a publisher headline. IPO source evidence remains attached to the edition's analyst inputs and decision log. `/storylog ANTHROPIC` filters those issuer decisions.
# Hourly research handoff

Approved results from the hourly Discovery Agent and separate Relevance Reviewer join cached publisher evidence. Model recommendations do not bypass original-source or materiality checks. `/researchdesk history` records discovery, source validation and relevance decisions; `/storylog` retains the brief editor's separate choices. IPO financial facts retain their source periods and units, and IPO stories have their own brief section. See `README_HOURLY_RESEARCH.md` for request limits, API billing, state and controls.

Event-aware editorial records use rules version 15. They freeze matching chart evidence and source-text priority profiles, then recompute priority during review. `/editorstatus` shows issuer event-check status and original company-calendar links; `/storylog` shows the retained story's editorial priority. A missing repeat check leaves a price cue pending. Existing rules-version records remain readable. Scheduling notices use a separate original-source calendar parser, so a routine call announcement can appear in What's Ahead without becoming material news.

New reader presentation records bind the opening takeaway and each plain-language watch signal to the same frozen research and retained original stories. Review recomputes the passages and requires complete, unchanged narration. `/editorstatus` identifies the opening and actual number of watch signals. This presentation changes no source gates, scoring weights, model request limits or delivery receipts.

Alphabet now has an original investor-relations RSS reader. GOOG and GOOGL share one issuer source and one editorial slot. A refresh reads the feed and at most one fresh exact release page; PDF-only results can use the unique document linked from that page under Alphabet's exact public CDN tenant. The complete heading, visible release date, PDF issuer and PDF title must match. The original zoned RSS publication clock is retained; fetch times, PDF creation dates and inferred California offsets never replace it. Failed reads leave body evidence unavailable and source coverage limited.

Alphabet results profiles use schema 5. Only the opening release summary can supply quarterly revenue, operating margin and earnings per share, with the quarter and year retained. Mixed-period tables, forecasts, adjusted figures and ambiguous duplicates are excluded. Schemas 1–4 keep their prior facts. The decision log retains both the issuer release page and its original document URL. Routine earnings-call notices remain calendar inputs rather than news stories.

When an Alphabet feed request is rate-limited, the structured reader preserves the fetcher's original HTTP status, observation time and retry deadline. HTTP 429 waits at least 15 minutes; a numeric publisher `Retry-After` can extend that to six hours. Forced refreshes respect the same deadline. A malformed successful response retains the separate five-minute parse-failure retry. These retries do not extend an article's publication window.

Original company reports of awarded, won, secured or received task orders are classified as contract developments, including singular and plural task orders without the word "contract" in the headline. They do not depend on an earlier hourly relevance approval remaining current. Task-order guides, opinions, forecasts and negated awards do not establish an award. Original-source, publication-age, observation-age and diversity checks remain in force; an "up to" amount stays a contract ceiling rather than funded commitments or booked revenue. Saved editions retain their original decisions, and the next build applies the corrected classification.

Dated Alphabet earnings context is separate from fresh-story admission. Only a matching rechecked move of at least five percent can activate it, and its company must remain in that attempt's focus selection. A retained fresh earnings story suppresses duplicate context. Original issuer results can be read up to 72 hours from publication, while still being held as news after 24 hours. The independent SEC route verifies CIK 1652044, Alphabet's primary inline identity and share classes, Item 2.02, a unique same-accession Exhibit 99.1, release date and stated results period. SEC acceptance and original release dates remain separate. Known amendments, conflicting original figures, stale observations and changed evidence withhold context. The general SEC/news publisher-authority rules and hourly model/search/read budgets are unchanged.

Context is frozen with the analyst evidence and revalidated for the exact edition. It replaces the matching company's generic price-follow-up paragraph, remains subject to the 3,800-character and narration checks, and never changes numerical rankings. The source log labels it `DATED EARNINGS CONTEXT` and excludes it from fresh-story counts. Earlier editions without the new evidence retain their original narration and decisions.
