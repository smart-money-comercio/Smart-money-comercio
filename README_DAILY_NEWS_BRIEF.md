# Smart Money Daily News Brief

For a same-provider rechecked Alphabet move of at least five percent, Companies in Focus can now use a clearly labeled recent earnings context paragraph. Context is limited to 72 hours from the original publisher clock, or from the separately labeled SEC acceptance clock. A SEC filing clock never substitutes for the release's publication time. The release date, quarter and year remain explicit, and the paragraph does not establish the cause of the price move. Context does not count as a fresh selected story; the news editor still uses its 24-hour window.

The normal news refresh also checks Alphabet's SEC submissions and at most one results 8-K and its uniquely linked Exhibit 99.1, for a maximum of three original-source requests. It uses the existing `SEC_USER_AGENT` administrative contact, with `SEC_API_USER_AGENT` as an alternate; example contact values are not sent. No additional model or search requests are made. Rendering remains cache-only. `/editorstatus` shows backup health, while `/storylog GOOG` and `/storylog GOOGL` retain the release, filing, observation clock and period-specific facts. Failed refreshes may retain explicitly labeled, previously verified context for at most six hours from its original observation; retrying does not reset that clock.

`/report`, `/brief` and `/newsbrief` return a concise news edition suitable for reading aloud and later adapting for YouTube or a podcast. The existing scheduled daily delivery and `/rundailyagent` use that same edition. This update creates text only; it does not generate audio, publish a video or create another scheduled send.

The edition is capped at 3,800 characters and uses short paragraphs under Market Update, Companies in Focus, Global and Defense Watch, What's Ahead, What We're Watching and Research Notes. It keeps the report's generation time and the quoted regular-session date separate. Weekend and after-hours prices are described as a completed session. Returns without a dated quote session are withheld. Watchlist breadth is explicitly watchlist breadth rather than a claim about the whole market. TLT remains a bond-price proxy, not verified Treasury-yield data.

Every delivered news edition must contain at least 180 spoken words, estimating a minimum of 60 seconds at the reference pace of 180 words per minute. The count excludes the title, dates, generation metadata, section headings, command links and Research Notes footer. `/reportcheck` shows the spoken word count, estimated seconds and the minimum-duration check. Actual audio duration depends on the voice and delivery pace; a later audio export can measure the finished recording.

The spoken-copy extractor recognizes all report greetings, including `Hello.` before 05:00 in the report's local timezone. Regression checks render the edition at every local hour and retain the true 179/180-word boundary overnight; display metadata and research links still do not contribute to the minimum.

Market Update has its own 15–30 second check: 45–90 spoken words at the same reference pace. The renderer prefers no more than 75 words for delivery at a more measured pace. It combines dated broad indexes, volatility and relevant oil/bond observations with an indicator-based sentiment read and a conditional portfolio lens. Less central figures can remain in `/fullreport`. Watchlist participation is discussed separately and does not determine broad-market sentiment. Missing market observations produce a clearly limited read. Headings, commands and URLs do not count toward either narration check.

The portfolio lens also considers the original-source stories actually retained in the edition: monetary policy, trade restrictions, energy and geopolitical developments can change the questions for growth exposure, supply chains, costs and defense. These are explicitly conditional interpretations, not measured subscriber portfolio returns or claims about the cause of price moves. A story removed for length also loses its derived interpretation. Each retained headline and its publisher/date remain available in the spoken edition and decision record.

Global and Defense Watch covers admitted agency/company reports and innovations, including drones, counter-drone systems, cyber defense and autonomy. Technical demonstrations remain distinct from funded orders, production readiness and revenue. A quiet or incomplete news window is identified without inventing a defense announcement. The normal source refresh now includes Lockheed Martin's newsroom RSS and DARPA's official news RSS; Lockheed company stories still need the issuer in the covered universe. Source outages, unverified clocks and old publications retain the existing hold rules.

The brief now carries the analyst agent's interpretation of the same validated research edition. It leads with a dated market tension, explains watchlist participation, identifies meaningful leadership and opportunity-score changes, compares the leading company's price support with its ranking, and carries forward that company's setup risk and trade-plan confirmation. Scheduled releases include a short explanation of their investor relevance. Original company reports receive follow-up questions appropriate to earnings, guidance, contracts or the other verified development, rather than the same product-launch sentence for every story.

Observed figures, model rankings and conditional interpretation remain distinct. The analyst reads the existing report; it performs no new source fetch, rescoring, position sizing or trade execution. It excludes undated company price moves, estimated earnings dates and free-form headline claims from its analysis. The edition record retains the exact analyst inputs and passages, matched to the underlying report digest. `/editorstatus` identifies those inputs and `/storylog` retains the separate source decisions. A quiet news day can still explain the agent's research priorities without inventing a company catalyst.

The top two research entries now include the adjustments captured during that ranking run: price, headline activity, volume, score risk and structural-score revision. Where all four underlying model scores exist, the detailed report also retains core, defense/theme, congressional and insider readings. The brief checks that the rounded contributions reconcile with the displayed opportunity score before using them to explain a leader's advantage over the runner-up. It identifies narrow leads, displayed ties, capped scores and a runner-up with stronger structural support. It does not infer the missing adjustments from a score gap or treat model scores as fresh transactions, financial results or verified business developments.

The narration explains those inputs in ordinary language; the exact values remain in `/fullreport` and the edition's evidence record. Optional model-support and company-price passages can make room for a complete verified news story while preserving the ranking explanation, source attribution, narration minimum and character limit. An absent or inconsistent breakdown leaves the existing qualified research summary in place.

The free brief provides useful conclusions and points readers to the existing full research edition for rankings, trade-plan conditions and risk checks. It makes no claim that a paid membership or restricted research service is already available. Membership and payment features are separate from this editorial update.

When more context is needed, the builder first includes additional publisher-verified calendar entries, then derives index-return comparisons or watchlist participation percentages from the same dated observations. It does not add unconfirmed stories or repeated filler. Whole-story selection and optional paragraph trimming must satisfy both the narration floor and the character cap. If the available evidence cannot support both, the build fails with a narration-quality reason and no passing edition is recorded.

Deployment checks use explicit `--offline-fixture` inputs for the report, daily-agent and intelligence quality scripts. These exercise the actual renderer and duration validator against isolated synthetic data; they do not publish a report, overwrite live agent status or relax the production narration requirement. The default quality commands continue to validate current runtime inputs.

The writing follows a market-review pattern inspired by the supplied Empower commentary: start with the main observation, give the dated numbers, and close with a measured outlook. The opening and the "Our view" paragraph respond to the available session evidence. Outlook sentences pose follow-up questions about energy costs, a Nasdaq recovery or watchlist participation; they do not attribute price moves to an unverified cause. Empower's historical figures, private account information and investment opinions are not inserted into current news.

Company paragraphs keep original-source attribution, publication date/time and complete wording together. The spoken news edition requires dated company or agency reports with material investor relevance. Unconfirmed secondary headlines are held in `/storylog`; opinion, price predictions, stock-pick lists and retrospective profiles are skipped. Empty news slots remain empty. Numerical rankings and the conditional "Our view" outlook remain identified research context, separate from sourced news.

Unconfirmed Nasdaq earnings-date estimates remain in `/calendar`, `/catalysts` and `/fullreport`. They are excluded from spoken news. What's Ahead uses publisher-verified or still-valid previously verified calendar schedules, including future earnings calls and results releases confirmed in original company announcements. A call time is labeled as a call time. Missing coverage is identified rather than replaced with estimated timing.

Company names are expanded for the core coverage and common research leaders, including Apple, Alphabet, Amazon, Northrop Grumman and Lockheed Martin. Complete source headlines are retained in narration; the editor holds incomplete wording and removes whole stories to meet the length limit. Retrospectives and personal-wealth profiles are excluded from the daily company-news slots unless the headline establishes a relevant corporate event such as compensation or a share sale. These editorial rules do not change company scores or independently confirm headline claims.

The news edition is rendered from the same validated detailed report. `/fullreport` retains the research tables, scores, trade-plan snapshot, allocation, source context and validation commands. Quality checks recognize both formats; the news edition has its own structure, date/greeting, length and source-qualification checks. It does not present numerical research rankings as confirmed entries or change stock scores. Important cached market alerts retain their dated source evidence and do not establish the cause of a price move.

Ranking entries retain their complete model evidence, Score Risk and review commands when the detailed report is formatted. They are not cut by the older fixed line limit. Candidate order, risk consistency and the overall character limit are still checked; a failed build names the failing research check. Offline deployment fixtures exercise the production ranking enrichment through the detailed report builder before rendering the spoken brief.

The daily agent refresh now reaches direct chip, software, drone, cybersecurity and defense publishers as well as original release services. `/editorstatus` shows which configured sources were available, how many original reports had verified dates and how many were fresh before editorial selection. `/storylog SYMBOL` includes publisher coverage when no story qualifies. A quiet, healthy feed is separate from an unavailable source or a missing publication timezone. The 24-hour article age, six-hour observation age, materiality checks and both narration-duration checks remain enforced.

Conflicting dates are shown as conflicts, incomplete coverage is distinct from an empty result, headline-only reports remain unconfirmed by the company, and a market event is kept separate from company-specific news. The analyst agent explains the next research checks without adding score changes, risk votes or symbol votes. Fresh price and volume confirmation still require a separate check.

Calendar Review and direct report builds now warm five business days, so later-week catalysts can feed the brief, existing trade plans and scorecards. Company brief readers fetch no network data, do not rescore, do not send to a channel, and open the existing SQLite event database read-only. A missing database is not created by the reader. Calendar age, ranking age and event expiry retain their existing policies.

The check reparses original chart prices, the dated prior close, session identity and split evidence. A same-provider repeat is explicitly qualified; it is not independent corroboration. Conflicting, stale, missing or corporate-action evidence leaves the check pending and suppresses the large numeric move from spoken company commentary. Repeat checks can be reused for 15 minutes. The original research quotes and numeric scores are unchanged.

Fresh original earnings, guidance and regulatory disclosures with usable source text can lead the edition, ahead of older policy or product stories. A matching rechecked move raises editorial attention further without establishing causation. Company focus can pair the admitted disclosure with the qualified market response. Source, publication-age and materiality gates still apply. The frozen edition retains its price proof, search cue and priority decision; prior editions keep their original rules.
